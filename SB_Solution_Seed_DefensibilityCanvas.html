Product Defensibility Score
Score your product's technical defensibility across the categories that hold up under diligence
Before you start: "better UX" will not hold up as a moat at Seed. Investors will push on whether a competitor with funding could rebuild what you have in a few months. This tool scores your product across five categories that actually survive that pressure-test, and tells you which one to invest in next.
1
What's actually shipped
What it is: the version of your product a customer can use today. How to calculate: use your actual current version label or release date, not the version you're planning to ship.
What it is: how long the current core product has been live with real customers. How to calculate: count months since your first paying (or seriously active) customer started using this version, not since you started building.
What it is: the features a customer can actually use right now. How to calculate: list only what's shipped and working, not what's on the roadmap below.
2
Your primary defensibility mechanism
Pick the one (or two) that's actually true for your business.
What it is: the mechanism that will make you harder to copy as you grow. How to calculate: pick the one your five scores below actually support, don't pick the one that sounds best if your data or network score is low.
What it is: a real, measured signal that the mechanism is already taking hold. How to calculate: use an actual before/after or cohort comparison from your own usage data, not a general industry claim.
3
Score your technical defensibility
Score each category 0 (none) to 5 (strong), based on what's true today, not your ambition.
/ 5
What it is: whether your product improves with data you accumulate that competitors can't easily get. How to calculate: score 0 (no data advantage) to 5 (clear, hard-to-replicate data moat).
/ 5
What it is: how hard your core technology is to rebuild from scratch. How to calculate: score 0 (easily rebuilt in weeks) to 5 (deep technical complexity or protected IP).
/ 5
What it is: how embedded your product becomes in a customer's existing workflow or systems. How to calculate: score 0 (standalone, easy to drop) to 5 (deeply integrated, costly to remove).
/ 5
What it is: whether the product gets better as more users join. How to calculate: score 0 (none) to 5 (strong, provable network effect).
