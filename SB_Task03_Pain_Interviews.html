Pain Interviews — 20+ Real People
Interviews completed 0 / 20
The Mom Test rule: Never present your solution. Ask about their life, not your idea. Every question should be about their past, not the hypothetical future. If they know what you're building, the data is contaminated.
Opening — 2 min
1.
"Thank you for your time. I'm researching how people like you deal with [topic area]. I'm not selling anything — I just want to learn from your experience."
2.
"Can you tell me about your role and what a typical week looks like for you?"
Discovery — 15 min
3.
"When was the last time you dealt with [problem area]? Walk me through exactly what happened."
Listen for: frequency, context, and who else was involved.
4.
"How did you handle it? What did you try?"
Current workarounds = your real competition.
5.
"What was the most frustrating part of that experience?"
6.
"How often does this happen to you?"
7.
"What have you tried to solve it? What worked, what didn't?"
If they pay for something now, that's strong signal.
8.
"If you could wave a magic wand and fix one thing about this, what would it be?"
Closing — 5 min
9.
"On a scale of 1 to 5, how painful is this problem for you right now?"
1 = minor annoyance · 5 = major blocker. You need 70%+ scoring 4 or 5 to pass the Stage 1 gate.
10.
"Is there anyone else in your network who deals with this same problem? Could you introduce me?"
The best interviews come from referrals — they come pre-validated.
!
What to avoid
Never say
"Would you use a product that…"
"Do you think this is a good idea?"
"Hypothetically, if we built…"
"I'm building something to help with…"
Instead ask
"Tell me about the last time…"
"How did you handle it?"
"What have you already tried?"
"How much did that cost you?"
