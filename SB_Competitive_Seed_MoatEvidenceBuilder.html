Moat Strength Score
Score your defensibility across the 5 categories investors actually check
Before you start: "defensibility" is one of the vaguest words in a pitch deck unless you break it into the specific categories investors actually probe: switching cost, network effects, data advantage, brand, and cost structure. Score each honestly, not aspirationally, since an inflated moat score is worse than a low one you can name a plan against.

Score your defensibility, 0 (none) to 5 (strong)
What it is: how painful it would be for a customer to leave you for a competitor. How to calculate: score 0 (trivial to switch) to 5 (deeply integrated, high switching pain), based on actual customer behavior.
What it is: whether the product gets better for each user as more users join. How to calculate: score 0 (none) to 5 (strong, provable network effect) based on real usage patterns, not aspiration.
What it is: whether your product improves with proprietary data you accumulate that competitors can't easily replicate. How to calculate: score 0 (no data advantage) to 5 (clear, hard-to-replicate data moat).
What it is: whether customers choose you specifically because they trust the brand, beyond features. How to calculate: score 0 (no brand pull yet) to 5 (customers actively seek you out by name).
What it is: whether you can serve customers profitably at a lower cost than competitors structurally. How to calculate: score 0 (no cost edge) to 5 (a real, defensible cost advantage, e.g. proprietary tech or supply access).
