MVP Solution Slide Builder
Turn a rough MVP into a credible Pre-Seed solution slide
Before you start: you don't need a finished product to build a strong solution slide, you need to be specific about what you have, honest about the stage it's at, and clear about what happens next. This tool assembles those pieces into a draft you can adapt directly, and scores your roadmap for how specific it actually is.

Your one-liner
Fill in the blanks. This mirrors the structure investors expect to see on this slide.
What it is: the name your MVP or product goes by right now. How to calculate: use your actual current product name, even if it's a working title, don't invent a polished name you haven't committed to.
What it is: the one-line market category a stranger would file you under. How to calculate: name the category the way an investor would write it on a deal sheet, not an aspirational label like "AI-powered everything platform".
What it is: the single concrete action your product performs. How to calculate: describe the actual mechanism your MVP does today (or will do at launch), not the long-term vision.
What it is: the specific person this is built for. How to calculate: name the narrowest group you're actually building for right now, pulled from your problem validation, not "everyone who could use this eventually".
What it is: the concrete result that persona gets. How to calculate: state the outcome as a measurable change (time saved, money saved, risk avoided), not a vague benefit.
What it is: the specific cost or frustration they avoid by using your product. How to calculate: name the actual painful workaround they use today that this replaces, pulled from your interviews.
2
Your product stage, stated honestly
Pick one. None of these need an apology at Pre-Seed.
What it is: where your build actually is today, not where you wish it were. How to calculate: pick "Idea" if nothing is built yet, "Prototype" if it's clickable but not functional end-to-end, "MVP" if a real user could complete the core action today.
What it is: proof that something exists beyond a slide. How to calculate: link, name, or describe exactly what a viewer can click through today, don't describe planned features as if they already exist.
3
Roadmap: next 6 months
Dated, specific milestones carry real weight when the product itself is still early.
What it is: a milestone with both a specific date and a concrete, named outcome. How to calculate: for the date field, enter an actual month (e.g. "Month 2"), not "soon" or "Q?". For the outcome field, name a concrete deliverable (e.g. "waitlist live with 50 signups"), not a vague plan like "make progress". Below, this is scored automatically.
4
Link back to the problem
What it is: the causal chain from your validated problem to this specific solution. How to calculate: reference the specific interview finding (quote or stat) this feature addresses, not a generic problem statement.
