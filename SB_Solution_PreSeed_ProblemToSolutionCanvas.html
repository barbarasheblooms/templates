Problem to Solution Canvas
Turn a validated problem interview insight into a testable solution hypothesis
Before you start: a validated problem tells you what's worth solving, not what to build. This canvas walks you from your interview insight, through How Might We questions, to two or three real solution concepts, and the smallest way to test one before you build it.
1
Your validated insight
Pull this straight from your problem interview notes, not from memory.
What it is: the specific segment you interviewed, not a broad market label. How to calculate: name the group the way your interview participants actually described themselves, pulled from your problem interview notes, not a generic persona.
What it is: the concrete obstacle blocking the outcome they want, ideally with a number attached. How to calculate: pull the exact quote or behavior from your interview transcripts, not a paraphrase, and include any number they mentioned (time lost, cost, failure rate).
What it is: the underlying goal behind the obstacle, in their words. How to calculate: ask "what were they actually trying to get done when this obstacle showed up," and answer using language from the interview, not your own framing of the solution.
2
Reframe as How Might We questions
Write 3 to 5 variants. Keep each one open enough to allow more than one answer.
What it is: an open reframing of the obstacle above, broad enough to allow multiple different solutions. How to calculate: take the obstacle from Section 1 and rewrite it starting with "How might we", removing any hint of a specific solution so you don't anchor on one answer too early.
3
Solution concepts
List 2 to 3 genuinely different concepts before picking a favorite.
What it is: a distinct way to answer one of your How Might We questions, not a feature variant of the same idea. How to calculate: for each concept, name the core mechanism in one sentence; if two concepts share the same mechanism, they're one concept, not two.
4
Smallest test before you build
What it is: the cheapest, fastest version of the chosen concept that still produces a real yes/no signal from real people. How to calculate: name a version you could run this week without writing production code (manual/concierge, a landing page, a mockup with a paid signup), and state what result would count as a pass versus a fail.
